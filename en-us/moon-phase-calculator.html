How to Use This Calculator

  1. The date starts on today — press Show Moon Phase to see tonight's Moon.
  2. Change the date to any day you like: your birth date to find the phase you were born under, or a future date to plan around a full moon.
  3. Read the illumination percentage and Moon age for the precise position in the cycle, and the countdowns for the next full and new moons.

How the Calculation Works

The calculator counts the days between your date (taken at noon UTC) and a known reference new moon — 6 January 2000 at 18:14 UTC — then divides by the average length of the lunar cycle, the synodic month of 29.530588853 days. The remainder is the Moon's age. Illumination follows from the geometry: illumination = (1 − cos(2π × age ÷ 29.530588853)) ÷ 2 × 100, which gives 0% at new moon, about 50% at the quarters and 100% at full moon. The phase name comes from which band the age falls into, using the eight standard bands in the table below.

An Honest Note About Accuracy

This is an average-cycle calculation. The real Moon speeds up and slows down in its elliptical orbit, so the exact clock time of a phase can differ from published almanac times by a few hours, and a date within a few hours of a phase boundary can be labelled with the neighbouring phase. For anything that depends on the exact hour, confirm with an astronomical almanac.

The 8 Moon Phases

One full cycle, in order. Ages are days since the new moon, using the same bands as the calculator.

New Moon0 – 1.85 daysThe Moon sits between Earth and the Sun; the lit side faces away and the Moon is dark in our sky. The cycle begins.

First Quarter5.54 – 9.23 daysHalf the face we see is lit. The Moon is a quarter of the way through its cycle — hence the name.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is this Moon phase calculator?

The phase name and illumination are typically accurate to within a few hours of published almanac values. The calculator uses the average length of the lunar month (29.530588853 days) counted from a known new moon, while the real Moon speeds up and slows down slightly in its elliptical orbit, so exact quarter times can differ by a few hours. For planning photos, fishing or a full-moon walk, it is comfortably close enough.

What Moon phase was I born under?

Enter your birth date above — the calculator works for any date, past or future. Many people enjoy learning their birth phase: a birth near the full moon means the Moon was almost fully lit, while a birth near the new moon means the cycle was just beginning. Try family members' birthdays too.

How often is there a full moon?

About once every 29.5 days — the length of one full cycle of phases, called a synodic month. Because that is slightly shorter than most calendar months, a calendar month occasionally gets two full moons; the second one is popularly called a blue moon. This calculator shows the exact number of days until the next full moon for any date you enter.

Why does the Moon change phase?

The phases are geometry, not the Moon changing shape. The Sun lights one half of the Moon at all times; as the Moon orbits Earth over about 29.5 days, we see different amounts of that lit half. At new moon the lit half faces away from us, at full moon it faces us fully, and the quarters and crescents are the in-between views.
