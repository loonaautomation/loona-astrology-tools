An Honest Note About January and February Births

The Chinese year starts at Lunar New Year — in late January to mid February — not on 1 January. So if you were born in January or February, and your birthday fell before that year's Lunar New Year, your animal is the previous year's animal, not the one for the calendar year on your birth certificate. This calculator cannot know every historical Lunar New Year date to the day, so for any January or February birthday it shows a clear warning and displays both animals: the calendar-year animal and the previous animal that applies if your birthday came before Lunar New Year. For birthdays from March to December the calendar-year animal is the correct one.

How It Works

The animal cycle repeats every 12 years and is anchored so that the year 4 CE was a Rat year: the animal index is (year − 4) mod 12, giving Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog and Pig in order. The element cycle repeats every 10 years from the same anchor: (year − 4) mod 10 gives Wood, Wood, Fire, Fire, Earth, Earth, Metal, Metal, Water, Water. Within each element pair, the first year is Yang and the second is Yin, alternating with the 10-year stem cycle. All of this is calculated with JavaScript on your own device — no server, no account.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is my Chinese zodiac sign?

Your Chinese zodiac sign is the animal of your birth year in the 12-year Chinese cycle: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog or Pig. Enter your birth date or year above and this calculator shows your animal, your element (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal or Water) and whether your year is Yin or Yang.

Why might January and February birthdays have a different animal?

The Chinese year does not start on 1 January. It starts at Lunar New Year, which falls between late January and mid February. If you were born in January or February before that year's Lunar New Year, you belong to the previous year's animal. This calculator warns you and shows both animals whenever a January or February birthday is entered.

What are the five Chinese zodiac elements?

Each year also carries one of five elements — Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal and Water — and each element rules two years in a row, the first Yang and the second Yin. The full cycle of 12 animals combined with 5 elements takes 60 years to repeat. Your element adds qualities to your animal: for example, 2024 was the year of the Wood Dragon, and its Yang side makes it the more outward, active version.

Is the Chinese zodiac the same as the Western zodiac?

No. The Western zodiac is based on the month and day you were born and follows the Sun through 12 signs in one year. The Chinese zodiac is based on the year you were born, follows a lunar calendar, and uses 12 animals with a 60-year cycle of animals and elements. Many people enjoy reading both, and the two systems describe completely different things.
