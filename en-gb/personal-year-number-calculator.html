How It Works

Reduce your birth month to one digit, reduce your birth day to one digit, and reduce the target year by adding its digits until one digit remains. Add those three numbers and reduce the total to 1–9 — that is your Personal Year. If the total is 11 or 22 before the final reduction, this page notes it as a master-number year but still gives the reduced digit as your Personal Year, so your place in the 9-year cycle stays clear. That convention is stated plainly here so you can compare like with like.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does my Personal Year start — on 1 January or on my birthday?

This calculator uses the most common convention: your Personal Year runs with the calendar year, from 1 January to 31 December. Some numerologists instead count from birthday to birthday, arguing the energy shifts on your birthday. Both views exist and neither is proven; if you use the birthday method, simply calculate with the year in which your most recent birthday fell and compare — many people find one method matches their experience better.

What is the 9-year cycle in numerology?

Numerology describes life as moving in repeating 9-year cycles, called epicycle years. Year 1 starts new ventures, the middle years build, change and consolidate them, and Year 9 completes and clears them away before the next Year 1 begins. Knowing your place in the cycle is meant to help you work with the year's theme instead of against it — planting in a Year 1, harvesting in a Year 8, and resting in a Year 7.

How is the Personal Year number calculated?

Add three reduced numbers together: your birth month reduced to one digit, your birth day reduced to one digit, and the target year reduced by adding its digits. Then reduce that total to 1–9. For example, born 15 May for 2026: month 5, day 15 → 6, year 2026 → 2+0+2+6 = 10 → 1; 5 + 6 + 1 = 12 → 3, so the Personal Year is 3. This page shows every step of your calculation.

What if my total is 11 or 22 — is that a master-number year?

Sometimes the three parts add to 11 or 22 before the final reduction. This page notes that as a master-number year — a year tradition describes as more intense and significant — but it still gives your Personal Year as the reduced digit (2 for 11, 4 for 22), because the 1–9 cycle themes are written for single digits. The note lets you know the extra intensity is there without changing the cycle position.
